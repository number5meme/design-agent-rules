# Product Showcase and Purchase Design Method

Arrange content around how users identify, compare, and choose products; brand expression, product differences, and purchase complexity jointly decide layout and visuals.

## Content and Layout

- Organize main image, name, price, specs, availability, and actions by decision needs; price, stock, and commitments use confirmed information.
- When users need to view images while choosing specs, a gallery beside a summary can be used; when value needs explaining first, a vertical narrative can be used; when comparison is emphasized, a comparison structure can be used. Decide proportions by content; do not fix a two-column desktop layout.
- Decide image proportions by subject and use, and sync the corresponding images when specs change; recheck cropping on mobile.
- Clearly distinguish option names and selected, out-of-stock, and unavailable states. The summary updates with selections and can be reviewed before submission; switching specs does not mean the purchase is complete.

## Visuals and Adaptation

Decide color, type, shape, and whitespace by brand and product, keeping action semantics consistent within the same task. Responsive layout is decided by the readability of images, options, and text; do not preset fixed column counts or breakpoints.

When a persistent purchase entry is needed, weigh a fixed summary's benefit against occlusion; when adopted, check safe areas, keyboard, and the last content. Verify long names, unavailable combinations, price changes, and error recovery.
