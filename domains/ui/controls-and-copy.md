## Operating Conventions and Expected Results

Routine tasks keep the interaction patterns familiar to the target platform and audience; changing conventions requires a clear experience benefit and discoverable cues, and users should not have to learn unrelated rules for ordinary operations. Stable operation semantics do not require identical layout, colors, or brand expression.

Choose controls by information type and operation purpose: single-select controls express mutually exclusive choices, multi-select controls allow choices to be combined, switches express on/off state, navigation changes location, and submit performs an explicit action. Distinguish when selection, editing, and actual effect take place; when a separate save is required, let the user know, and do not let a control's appearance suggest immediate effect while silently waiting for submission.

Before an operation, the name, appearance, and context should let users reasonably predict the target object, scope of impact, and result; distinguish expanding details, modifying data, and navigating to a page, and do not perform unrelated actions under a familiar appearance. Clarify uncertain business behavior per requirements; do not infer it from control styling.

Define the responsibility of the same field separately on display pages, edit pages, and quick actions. Read-only displays must not modify data directly because they reuse an edit component; when tapping into the edit page is agreed, check the behavior of the whole tap area and its child elements. Independent operations such as copy do not trigger parent navigation or modification; explicitly allowed inline editing may stay.

## Entry Naming and Action Consistency

- **Use a stable name for the same destination.** Locate and check affected related entry points, page titles, and result messages; do not swap synonyms arbitrarily by page. Actions and destinations may use different phrasing, but keep object names consistent; keep necessary distinctions for entry points whose target labels, identities, or behavior differ.
- **Exit entry points each have their own role.** Back follows the applicable navigation history or flow; hierarchical navigation goes to the named parent, which may differ from the previous location. Determine the destination from the actual entry path and platform conventions. Close exits the current pop-up, and Done ends editing or a confirmation step. When a clear Back/Close already exists, do not automatically add synonymous "Done", "Back to…", or bottom exit buttons. When Done carries saving, submitting, or flow completion, keep it or rename it to a specific action; when removing an exit button, also check unsaved changes, focus, and where the user returns.
- **Choose icon or text by recognizability.** Auxiliary actions with clear context that users easily recognize, such as back, close, copy, and share, may prefer icons; business actions, unfamiliar symbols, multiple possible objects, or high-consequence actions use text or icon plus text. Icon buttons keep an accessible name, sufficient hit area, and state feedback, and provide tooltips on desktop as needed.
- **Decide whether to add icons by recognition benefit.** For settings items, action menus, tool buttons, and category options, choose the presentation based on audience familiarity, content differences, and information density. Icons may be used with text when they help distinguish purposes or speed scanning; text alone may be used when text is clear enough or icons would add ambiguity or visual load. When using icons, match the object or action, and keep the text's explicit meaning.
- **Icons form a unified language.** Prefer reusing the project icon library; use the same icon for the same action, and keep size, stroke width, fill style, spacing, and optical alignment consistent within a group. Option-meaning icons, selected checks, and expand arrows serve identification, state, and navigation respectively; do not use decorative icons in place of state indicators. Repeated-meaning icons next to text are not announced again; standalone icon buttons provide an explicit name.
- **Unify semantics and presentation of auxiliary actions.** Within the same platform, actions with the same role, such as share and copy, reuse icons, containers, size, positioning logic, and feedback; toolbars and menus may present differently. A share entry means a share flow, and a copy entry specifies whether it copies an address, link, or other object; do not unify visually while performing different meanings.
- **Feedback reflects the result.** After a successful copy, indicate it was copied; on failure, provide a way to recover. Handle opening the share sheet, canceling a share, and actually completing a share separately; do not report a successful share just because the sheet opened.

When adding or modifying entry points, search related pages, pop-ups, and shared components by "destination/action/object", then check visible copy, icons, position, and behavior. When the project needs it, keep a brief action mapping in the design record, and maintain specific copy in a single primary record. After unifying, recheck applicable states for navigation, close, copy, and share; do not just do string replacement.

## Input Hints and Placeholder Content

- **Input hints provide incremental information.** Use input hints for brief examples, formats, or search scope; when the field's meaning is already clear, do not repeat the label, pile on instructions, or add hints to fill space. An already labeled nickname field needs no "Enter your nickname"; a search box may use a hint to state what can be searched.
- **Labels and necessary instructions stay visible.** Users still need to know the field's meaning after input; required conditions, units, limits, and errors are not placed only in the input hint. Compact contexts such as search may omit a visible label but keep clear context and an accessible name.
- **Hints do not serve as actual values.** Saved content goes into the field value; examples must not be submitted as data. Default values require a basis; fields such as amount, address, and quantity especially must distinguish examples, unknown, and a real zero.
- **Choose placeholders by state.** While loading, a skeleton matching the layout may be used; on load failure, provide a recovery entry; when there truly is no content, use an empty state or omit it. When assets such as avatars or covers are missing, use a fallback matching the content type; do not show unreplaced template text, and do not add a button for every empty field.
- **Design placeholders have a purpose.** Early drafts may use representative copy length, image proportions, and data shapes to check layout. When entering agreed delivery, replace temporary assets, and mark placeholders still allowed to remain in the project record; do not treat random images, QR codes, or sample balances as real content.

Check applicable states among empty, focused, filled, cleared, and error, confirming the meaning remains clear after the hint disappears. Placeholder text stays readable and distinct from input values, and long hints must not squeeze the control; keep tone, example format, and fallback consistent across fields of the same kind.

## Submission and Disclosure

- Do not disable submission solely because fields have not yet passed validation; let users submit to discover actionable errors. Real business restrictions, such as missing permission, unavailable service, or an incomplete prerequisite operation, still govern availability and need a clear explanation. On a submit rejected for field validation, identify errors beside their fields and focus the first invalid field; present non-field failures with a suitable recovery path. Guard against duplicate requests while a submission is pending.
- When navigation would discard unsaved edits, offer to stay or discard. Verify that leaving after a successful save does not trigger the warning.
- Let chip and tag collections wrap, or expose hidden items through an operable count control such as "+3". When a label must be truncated, verify that keyboard, pointer and touch users can each retrieve the full text.
