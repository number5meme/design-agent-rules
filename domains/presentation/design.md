# Presentation Design

Read by task: [Workflow](workflow.md) · [Method](design.md) · [Acceptance](quality.md). For local edits, read only the affected sections.

## Content and Narrative

Organize each slide around one main reading task: understanding a concept, comparing options, judging evidence, or taking action. The title states the slide's topic or a well-founded conclusion, with body text and charts providing support; reading the titles in sequence should convey the overall argument. Add an agenda, section dividers, and an ending as the content requires.

Set explanatory depth by use: live talks emphasize long-distance readability and speaking rhythm; standalone reading keeps the definitions, conditions, and basis needed to understand. When content is too dense, first remove duplication, group, or split slides; do not solve it by repeatedly shrinking the font.

Choose the organization by goal; not every deck needs all types:

| Goal | Content focus |
|---|---|
| Analysis and decision | Conclusions, evidence, comparison basis, trade-offs, and uncertainty |
| Proposal and reporting | Problem, value or progress, basis, risks, and next steps |
| Teaching and training | Concepts, demonstrations, exercises, and reasoned feedback |
| Research and engineering | Problem, method or structure, results, boundaries, and dependencies |
| Brand and portfolio showcase | Core expression, representative work, and necessary context |

## Slides and Visual System

Maintain the agreed aspect ratio, semantic colors, and chart annotations; cover, section, and content slides may use different margins, hierarchy, and composition, with repeated roles consistent within their own layouts. Choose comparison, process, structure diagram, table, or image layouts based on content relationships, keeping cross-slide consistency without forcing every slide to be identical. Maintain repeated elements per [Shared Components](../../standards/components.md).

Each slide should have a clear primary visual entry point; the main image, title, and notes should not compete for attention. Cards, decoration, spacing, and color serve grouping, emphasis, or expression; do not ban any layout outright, and do not add unrelated elements to fill the slide.

## Assets and Evidence

For specific products, scenes, experiments, and interfaces, prefer the corresponding real images or screenshots; concept illustrations are for explanation or mood and must not pose as field records or evidence. Confirm the content and aspect ratio of key assets first, then lay out around them, avoiding stretching or occluding the subject.

Choose chart representation by data relationship, and check units, time range, denominator, axes, legend, and source; distinguish inferences and assumptions from facts. When color expresses category or state, also provide text, shape, or position cues. Place the necessary basis and sources of citations on the corresponding slide or in the agreed reference area to keep them traceable.

Choose animation and transitions by presentation use; they may serve step-by-step explanation, narrative, brand, or emotional expression, without delaying reading or operation; reading and print uses must not depend on animation to understand the content. Step-by-step reveals should keep correct causality and reading order; speaker notes do not replace information needed for standalone reading.
