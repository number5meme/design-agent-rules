# Poster Design

Read by task: [Workflow](workflow.md) · [Method](design.md) · [Acceptance](quality.md). For local edits, read only the affected sections.

For promotional posters, event teasers, covers, and showcase images. Choose composition by placement purpose, content, and brand; do not presuppose people, a logo, buttons, or a fixed style.

## Information and Visual Hierarchy

- Determine the theme, main message, and the viewer's next action the image must convey. Organize title, main image, time and place, and supporting notes by information importance; do not add sections just to fill a template.
- Keep copy the user asked to preserve verbatim exactly as is; when polishing is allowed, keep the factual meaning. Names, titles, times, prices, benefits, and commitments must have a basis; when missing, confirm or omit optional content.
- Establish a clear priority between title and subject; font size, weight, position, color, and spacing together distinguish hierarchy. Check at the actual viewing size, not only on a zoomed-in canvas.
- Spacing between groups should reflect information relationships; keep alignment and rhythm within groups. Decoration serves the theme; do not add labels, small decorative English text, calls to action, or slogans by default.

## Assets and References

Distinguish portrait photos, product assets, logos, style references, and finished examples. The same image may play multiple roles, but confirm first when its use is unclear and would affect the result.

Analyze a reference's colors, typography, image-text relationship, compositional weight, materials, and mood, and state the dimensions adopted. When photos are used for identity or product accuracy, check that the subject's features are preserved; a reference style cannot automatically override these requirements.

In multi-person scenes, make explicit the correspondence between each photo and name and position, and check for omissions, duplicates, and mismatches. Fictional people must not pose as specified guests. Logos keep their proportions, clarity, and agreed colors; when exact reproduction is needed, use the valid provided assets directly.

## Composition and Format Adaptation

- Place text areas based on the real subject's position, gaze direction, copy length, and background detail. Composition may use columns, top-bottom relationships, centering, or overlap, as the content dictates.
- Copy should avoid faces, product details, and high-contrast textures that affect recognition; intentional overlap must keep both subject and text clear.
- When switching between landscape and portrait or changing aspect ratio affects reading order, reorganize content and spacing. Check multiple sizes separately; do not substitute overall stretching or fixed-coordinate scaling for adaptation.
- Re-layout ultra-wide, ultra-tall, and small covers by the short side, actual display size, and content volume; avoid squeezing a regular poster into an unreadable thumbnail. Before cropping, define the areas to keep, and check people, logos, and key text.
- Handle an oversized subject and an off-center framing separately: adjust size and position for an editable subject; redo the asset when the whole base image needs recomposition. Cropping only changes framing; it cannot restore subject content that is already missing.

## Typography, Layers, and Production Method

Use fonts that are actually available and suit the purpose; check Chinese and English text, numerals, punctuation, missing glyphs, and line breaks. Do not ban any category of font outright, and do not prescribe all font sizes by a uniform ratio.

Choose direct layout, asset compositing, vector drawing, or image generation based on accuracy, editability, and visual result. When exact text, logos, or editable delivery is needed, keep the corresponding content as separate objects; when only a final image is needed and the generated result meets the requirements, the image may be delivered directly.

Plan subject and text space before generating assets, and correct based on the actual image afterward. Background style and subject rendering may be constrained separately, but realistic, illustrated, collage, and similar treatments should follow the chosen direction. A strong style or specific scene must not be silently replaced with another style.

For editable delivery, organize layers by canvas so that text, logos, and other independently editable content can be located; key objects are included within the correct export bounds. When merging or rasterizing is needed, keep the agreed source-file capabilities.
