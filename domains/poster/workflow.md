# Poster Production Workflow

Read by task: [Workflow](workflow.md) · [Method](design.md) · [Acceptance](quality.md). For local edits, read only the affected sections.

Posters, covers, and showcase images follow this workflow; for the design method, see [Poster Design](design.md), and when done, check per [Poster Acceptance](quality.md). Confirm the delivery format per [Task Scope](../../standards/task-scope.md).

## 1. Identify Inputs and Necessary Gaps

Clarify purpose, aspect ratio, content, asset roles, and editability requirements; use existing information directly. Confirm only gaps that affect the result; judge people, logos, and copy as the task requires, without a fixed list of mandatory questions.

When the user will supply assets later, pause production that depends on them; information organization and independent composition work may continue. A person's name does not mean a photo exists, nor is it a basis for generating their likeness.

## 2. Determine the Design Direction

| Input | Handling |
|---|---|
| Explicitly specified reference image | Extract the visual characteristics the user asked for, and identify business and brand content in the image that must not be copied |
| Complete style rules | Identify canvas, subject, text, background, decoration, and hierarchy by semantics, and check whether they fit this content and aspect ratio |
| Only a theme or style keyword | Form a recommended direction from audience, content, and placement environment; compose autonomously, adopting only applicable dimensions when a reference is needed |

Reconcile multiple inputs by explicit requirements and the selected template; confirm material conflicts first. When the style is rejected, redetermine the direction per [Style Adjustment](../../standards/workflow.md#style-adjustment). Complete rules are adequate when they can guide this composition and layout; specific fields, tool syntax, or template formats are not required.

For multiple matching materials of the same subject or brand, maintain the baseline and variable items per [Series Visuals](../../standards/series-visuals.md).

## 3. Choose a Production Method and Produce

Choose a layout, compositing, drawing, or generation route based on delivery requirements and existing assets, confirming required tool capabilities first. When image generation is needed, plan subject, background, and text areas in advance; returned size and effect follow the actual result, and do not assume the service supports a fixed size table.

Map title, subject description, time and place, and other information by semantics; skip missing optional items. Keep confirmed facts and copy, and maintain readability when adjusting width, line breaks, arrangement, and font size.

For multi-person or multi-asset tasks, establish explicit correspondences. After a reference fails, do not silently drop key assets, replace people, or lower editability requirements.

## 4. Correct Based on the Actual Image

View the real output, check subject position, background detail, text space, and reading order, then adjust the image-text relationship. Choose a local fix, re-layout, or redone asset based on the defect; do not accept a substandard result because a fixed number of attempts was reached.

When there is no new evidence or feasible fix, state the blocker and impact, and continue independent work. Handle async status and retries per the [Workflow](../../standards/workflow.md#async-operations-and-retries).

## 5. Export and Delivery

Check each agreed spec per [Poster Acceptance](quality.md), and deliver the corresponding final images and source files. Record verified results and remaining gaps, using a brief record for small tasks; do not add web pages, prototypes, or full product documentation.
