# Brand and Character Design

Read by task: [Workflow](workflow.md) · [Method](design.md) · [Acceptance](quality.md). For local edits, read only the affected sections.

## Design Basis

Learn the brand name, purpose, audience, and existing assets; gather only information that affects the result. For an existing logo or character, first check whether it serves as the current baseline; handle keeping, local adjustment, and redesign separately. Translate the brand concept into reasons for form and typography; base style and colors on the specific positioning rather than mechanically applying industry conventions.

Check the brand name and wordmark for spelling, language, and capitalization. When naming or copywriting is not requested, do not add brand names or slogans; awards, certifications, partner marks, and product claims require a real basis.

When the brand direction is rejected, handle it per [Style Adjustment](../../standards/workflow.md#style-adjustment), distinguishing this visual style from brand identity assets that must be retained.

## Logo

- Determine symbol, wordmark, or combination form by purpose; direction exploration should compare different construction ideas, not use color changes as different concepts. The number of options follows the agreement; a recommended option by default is sufficient.
- Check outline, negative space, proportion, letter spacing, and optical alignment. Check recognizability and structure at the actual minimum application size and on applicable single-color and reversed backgrounds.
- Deliver the final logo and showcase mockups separately: the logo in scenes must not be distorted, misspelled, or redesigned. Mockups cannot replace the agreed standalone logo files.
- When vector or transparent background is required, verify actual paths, the alpha channel, and export results; a bitmap with a vector look cannot serve as a vector source.

## Mascots and Characters

Determine archetype, silhouette, proportions, signature features, and personality from the purpose, then choose expressions, poses, and medium. Features should be recognizable and reproducible; choose realistic, flat, 3D, or other styles as required.

Produce the views, expressions, or materials that depend on the main character only after it is settled. Produce only agreed content; do not default to turnaround sheets, sticker packs, or merchandise. Across views, check body shape, facial features, patterns, accessories, and left-right relationships; do not change the character's identity for a new pose. Extensions of existing characters use real baseline assets; for the method, see [Series Visuals](../../standards/series-visuals.md).

## Visual System and Applications

When the scope includes a visual system, maintain a readable primary record of colors, typography, logo combinations, clear space, and graphic language. Applied materials reuse these rules and re-layout per carrier; color and typography notes must match the actual assets.

When the baseline changes, keep constraints that were not modified; check results per [Brand Acceptance](quality.md).
